## 12. DCC builder and assistant (built 2026-10-04)

the owner's request: a D&D Beyond style creator for DCC, from first roll to level-up, with a guide overlay, a "What is this?" button backed by a local model, background music, and a parchment theme. the owner builds the character sheet himself (`dcc_sheet.html` is his; it still extends the old `base.html`).

| Part | File | Notes |

| Rules | `ttrpgtools/dcc.py` | Audited against the printed core rulebook on 2026-10-04, see below |
| JSON interface | `ttrpgtools/web/dcc_routes.py`, lower half | `/TTRPG/dcc/api/...` |
| Builder | `static/dcc_builder.js`, `templates/dcc_builder.html` | `/TTRPG/dcc/new` (0-level rolls), `/TTRPG/dcc/<id>/build` (shop, languages, level-up), `/TTRPG/dcc/<id>/level-up` |

### Theme: Moebius palette (2026-10-04, replaces the parchment look of the same day)

the owner uploaded twelve basic-land cards with Jean Giraud art to a folder on the file share and asked for their pastel look. The palette is sampled from those images and lives as `--m-*` variables at the top of `static/dcc.css`: six fields (sage, aqua, clay, citron, lilac, ash), nine lettering colors, six picture colors, four neutrals. `/TTRPG/dcc/palette` shows every swatch with its variable name and hex value.

A page takes one "land" through `<html data-land="...">`: `--field` (page color), `--word` (large headings), `--word-ink` (the same color darkened until small text reads on paper), `--rule` (the line under headings). `LAND` in `web/dcc_routes.py` picks the land by class (cleric island, thief wastes, warrior and dwarf mountain, wizard swamp, elf forest, halfling and 0-level plains); list page forest. Fonts: Josefin Sans for headings and labels, Bebas Neue for the masthead and big numbers, Alegreya for body text (all OFL, in `static/fonts/`).

Textures: `body::before` multiplies `--texture` over the field. Default is a generated grain (`dcc_grain.svg`). the owner will supply textures: put the file in `static/textures/`, set `--texture`, `--texture-size`, `--texture-strength` in `:root` (README there).

`dcc_sheet.html` now extends `dcc_base.html` (the owner reported the old dark look, 2026-10-04); its classes are styled at the end of `dcc.css` until his illustrated sheet replaces it.

### Interface for the sheet

- `GET /TTRPG/dcc/api/character/<id>` returns `{id, character, view}`. `character` is the stored file; `view` is everything computed (`ac`, `saves`, `initiative`, `init_die`, `attack`, `action_dice`, `crit_die`, `crit_table`, `fumble_die`, `speed`, `weapons`, `thief_skills`, `spellcasting`, `spells`, `languages`, `rolls` with named parts, `warnings`, `todo`, `class_extras`).
- `POST /TTRPG/dcc/api/character/<id>/op` with `{"op": ...}` changes one thing and returns the new state. Ops: `hp` (delta), `xp` (value), `burn_luck` (points), `recover_luck`, `spellburn` (strength, agility, stamina), `rest` (bed_rest, prefer), `disapproval` (delta), `ability_damage`, `buy` (key, count, pay, mithril), `add_item`, `remove_item`, `equip`, `qty`, `coins`, `spells`, `mercurial` (spell, roll), `choices` (lucky_weapon, lucky_spell, deity, patron), `languages`, `set` (name, alignment, notes).
- A page that extends `dcc_base.html` gets the theme and the three assistant buttons. `data-explain="..."` on an element feeds the rulebook search; `DCC.context = () => ({...})` passes character facts to the model; `DCC.guide = () => ({id, steps: [{target, text}]})` defines the page's guide.

### Explain

`python -m ttrpgtools.dcc_explain build <core rulebook pdf or reading-order txt>` builds the index (1,722 passages; section headings become titles, player chapters outrank spell and monster pages). `answer()` sends the clicked element's HTML, nearby text and the best passages to the first reachable llama.cpp server of `DCC_EXPLAIN_HOSTS` (default `llamacpp-9b:8080,llamacpp-4b:8080`) and streams the reply. It never starts a model: one GPU group at a time. Without a model the user sees the closest rulebook passage instead.

### Music

Files in `content/dcc/music/` (gitignored, bind-mounted) play in order. `CREDITS.txt` there is shown in the button's tooltip. Seeded with three Kevin MacLeod tracks (CC BY 4.0).

The earlier check counted rows only; the class tables are now compared value by value against the core book and match.

Rulings where the book leaves room (one constant or one line each to change). the owner confirmed 1, 2 and 6 on 2026-10-04; standing rule from him: the core rulebook outranks the reference booklet, sheets and any other source.

8. An occupation shortbow comes with 1d6 arrows (footnote says "such as sling or dart").

## 13. The west marches campaign: town, shops, inns, traders (built 2026-10-04)

One DCC campaign, west marches style: time runs 1:1 with the real calendar and players act in town during downtime. the owner: "Only one campaign so we can hardcode a lot." Everything specific to the campaign lives in the package `ttrpgtools/dcc/westmarch/`; more campaign tracking will be added there.

| Part | File |
|---|---|
| Campaign facts written by hand: start date, time zone, admin, places with shelves and services, traders | `ttrpgtools/dcc/westmarch/places.py` |
| Calendar, prices, buying, selling back, deliveries, town news | `ttrpgtools/dcc/westmarch/town.py` |
| Daily update: deliveries and the traders (local model or plain rule) | `ttrpgtools/dcc/westmarch/daily.py` |
| Routes `/TTRPG/dcc/town`, `/api/town`, `/api/town/buy` (buy and sell), `/api/town/judge` | `ttrpgtools/web/dcc_routes.py` |
| Page | `static/dcc_town.js`, `templates/dcc_town.html` |
| What play changed (quantities, judge edits, sales log, news, traders' purses) | `data/westmarch/town.json` (bind-mounted, gitignored), lock file next to it |

- **Calendar**: campaign day 1 is `places.EPOCH` (2026-10-04); a new day starts at midnight in Europe/Berlin.
- **Places**: five, all placeholders until the owner names them: The Gilded Goose (inn and shop), Hammer & Tongs (shop), The Crooked Shelf (shop), The Drowned Rat (inn), The Silver Stag (inn). `places.py` is merged into the state on every load: a new place or item appears, quantities in play are kept, an item the admin removed stays removed.
- **Price** on a day = usual price x place wave x item wave, each `1 + amplitude * sin(2 pi (day + phase) / period)`, whole copper, at least 1 cp. Curves nobody set are derived from the names: place swing 10 to 30 percent over 18 to 40 days, item swing 20 to 60 percent over 5 to 17 days. The admin can set each curve and sees four weeks of it.
- **Shelves** are finite and shared; a quantity of None never runs out. Ammunition trades per bundle. Services (lodging, meals) cost coin and are written to the character's `downtime` list instead of the pack.
- **Selling back**: a shop pays `places.SELL_BACK` (half) of its own price today for goods of the kinds it deals in. The value comes from its shelf, else the rulebook tables, else what the owner paid; unpriced things need the admin. Sold goods land on that shelf for everyone.
- **Deliveries**: each shop has `restock_every` days; on its delivery day every item below the quantity written in `places.py` is refilled to it. Goods that players or traders sold to the shop are not refilled.
- **Traders** (`places.BOTS`, placeholders): each has a purse and pack in the town file, earns `income` copper a day, brings some of its own wares, and takes at most three actions through the same `buy` and `sell` as a player. The local model (`DCC_EXPLAIN_HOSTS`, llamacpp-9b then llamacpp-4b) chooses the actions and one remark; when no model answers, a plain rule plays (sell own wares where they pay best, sell earlier purchases only at a profit, buy one bargain over 10 percent below usual). Purchases unsold after 14 days leave town.
- **Daily update** runs in a background thread on the first town visit after midnight, or by hand with `python -m ttrpgtools.dcc.westmarch.daily`. Missed days (up to 14) are caught up with the plain rule. The model is asked outside the file lock.
- **Admin** is the login `admin` (`places.JUDGES`). Only the admin sets purses, grants starting gold or uses the rulebook price list in the builder. A request without a login name (local run) counts as admin. Players see only their own characters as shoppers.

### Added later on 2026-10-04

Shelves, trader packs and character packs are loaded from it into plain lists and written back; character files no longer hold an inventory (`inventory_in_table`). Admin view: "All items" on the town page.
- **Places**: Shop1 (outfitter and plain inn), The Blacksmith, Shop3 (Groderick buys anything at 15 percent of today's value, 2 cp flat for unpriced things), The Shoddy Inn, The Good Inn. The town page lists the places; a click opens one.

- **Players see today's price only.** Usual price, trend and curves are admin-only, except that a character whose occupation is listed under a trade good's `knows` sees that good's curve (at most `INSIGHT_MAX`). Five trade goods in `places.TRADE_GOODS`, three are placeholders.
- **Inns**: `stay` per place (price, hp, ability). Nights are paid up front; `town.settle` applies passed real nights whenever a character is loaded. Shoddy and Shop1: 1 and 1 (the rulebook's night of rest); good: 2 and 2 (bed rest).

- Loot: open. the owner wants to think about how the DM hands it out.
- **Three moons** (`places.MOONS`, `town.moon`): the Pale Moon is the real moon; the Red Lantern runs 13 nights and is full for 5; the Drowned Moon runs 61 nights, dark for 28, full for 1. A character is bound to one at creation (first builder step, required); only the admin changes it later. Shown on the town page, the character list and in the character API (`moon`).
- **Bot scripts**: a trader with `script` (text file in `westmarch/bots/`) and `limits` in `places.BOTS` is "scripted". `daily.candidates` lists the only moves inside the limits (sell above what was paid by `min_profit`, buy at or below `buy_below` of the usual price, one piece per line, pack not full); the script is the model's instruction and the model answers with line numbers; `scripted_plan` and `act` cut any answer to the limits, and without a model the best lines are taken. `bots/arbitrage.txt` plays Vessa the factor: 2 buys and 2 sells a day, pack of 6, starts with 40 gp, no income. `python -m ttrpgtools.dcc.westmarch.daily simulate <days> [bot id] [model]` plays future days on a copy and prints the purses; nothing is saved. Measured 2026-10-04: 40 gp to 171 gp in 120 days with the plain rule, 40 gp to 47 gp 8 sp in 6 days with the model.
- **Tax debt** is 10,000 gp per character (a million copper), after the owner's "like a minimum wage job but owing a million".
- **Villagers are the roster** (2026-10-04): `villagers.py` holds ten locked TOWNSFOLK (keepers, the mayor, the four traders) and ninety playable VILLAGERS. `registry.roll_villager` rolls each playable one a 0-level character once, seeded by the name, stored as `people.sheet`. A player creates a character with `registry.claim` (alignment and moon chosen, 10 XP given): the villagers page shows abilities, hit points and gear but never the birth augur (the admin sees it). `/TTRPG/dcc/new` leads there; the roll-your-own wizard remains at `/TTRPG/dcc/new?roll=1`, and the 0-level party roller at `/TTRPG/dcc/funnel`, both without links. A character's death or return sets the villager's status.
- **Nightly update at 02:00**: `places.UPDATE_HOUR`; systemd user units `~/.config/systemd/user/westmarch-daily.{service,timer}` run `docker exec sheet python -m ttrpgtools.dcc.westmarch.daily` every night (Persistent). A town visit after that hour is the fallback.
- **Moons, told to players**: name, a three-word vibe and tonight's phase. Cycle lengths and countdowns are admin-only (the owner: "just the names and a quick brief vibe").
- **Taxes for no reason** (the owner's joke, 2026-10-04): in the builder, a number whose Roll button was used and that is then raised by hand adds `places.TAX_SURCHARGE` (6,000 gp) to the character's tax debt and shows a full-screen notice; lowering it takes 1 cp off; raising it again costs the surcharge again (`fiddled` in `dcc_builder.js`, operation `taxes_for_no_reason`). A number typed without rolling costs nothing.
- **Second rolls double the debt** (the owner, 2026-10-04): a Roll button used a second time on the same number in the builder (`rerolled`, operation `reroll`), and "Roll the birth augur again" (operation `reroll_augur`, `dcc.reroll_augur`, 0-level only, hit points follow the augur). Every debt figure on the page blinks and counts up to the doubled sum (`showDebtJump`).
- **Money notation**: gold with two decimals, like cents, German separators: 1002 cp is "10,02 gp", a million copper "10.000,00 gp" (`town.coins_text`, `DCC.gp`). `dcc.cost_in_cp` reads that notation back; a comma or dot followed by exactly three digits is a thousands separator ("1,200 gp" from the rulebook tables still works).
- **Explain and the campaign**: `dcc_explain.HOUSE_NOTES` holds what the rulebook cannot know (tax office, money, shops, inns, moons, villagers), registered in `westmarch/__init__.py`. A click on something of the campaign is answered from those notes, not from rulebook pages. The model wait is 60 seconds per host, so the CPU model can answer when the GPU one is down.

## 15. Death, the library, the town's debt and interest (2026-10-05)

- **The town owes, not only the players** (`westmarch/debts.py`): every villager starts with `places.TAX_DEBT`. A villager nobody plays carries the debt in `people.debt_cp`; when a player takes the villager over, the debt moves into the character (`tax_debt_cp`) and the row is set to 0. The counter on the character list is `debts.town_total()`: all characters that are not dead plus all unplayed villagers that are not dead.
- **Death** (operation `die`): everything the character carried goes to the library (`town.bequeath`), the debt is split evenly among everyone else alive (`debts.spread`, odd coppers to the first in line, nothing lost), the villager is marked dead. The same split happens when the admin marks an unplayed villager dead. Undoing a death (`revive`) takes the same sum back from everyone (`debts.gather`); the things stay at the library. The dead character's coins are left untouched (open question).
- **The library** (`places.PLACES`, kind `library`): its shelf is a holder in the items table like any shop's. `town.borrow` moves one piece into a pack marked `on_loan`; `town.give_back` returns it. A loan cannot be sold; nothing is charged; there is no limit and no due date. Traders ignore the library.
- **Interest** (`debts.accrue`, called first thing in the nightly update, never in simulations): each night a debt grows by `INTEREST_MONTHLY / 30.44 x weight(phase) / mean weight of that moon's cycle`. Weights in `places.INTEREST_BY_PHASE` run from 0 at new moon to 2 at full. Averaged over whole cycles every moon gives 2 percent a month; the Drowned Moon adds nothing for 28 dark nights and more than four times the average near its one full night. A character uses the moon they chose; unplayed villagers and older characters get one from their name (`debts.moon_of`). Players are not told that the moon matters.
- Tests now give every test its own character folder: the nightly interest walks all characters, and a test had reached for the real files (it could not write them).

## 16. Money as a closed system, and the Luck nudge (2026-10-05)

the owner: "we keep the money amount in the village tracked. This is now among other things a small macroeconomics experiment, where we control the money-supply via the Finanzamt and a bank."

- **Holders of coin**: character purses, the copper rolled for villagers nobody plays, trader purses, a till per shop and inn (`till` in `places.PLACES` is the opening amount), the bank's vault, the Finanzamt (`state["money"]`).
- **Play only moves coin** (`westmarch/town.py`): buying, a night at an inn and services pay into the place's till; a shop buys only what its till can pay; taxes go to the Finanzamt; a dead character's coins go into the library's cash pot (`town.pot_the_purse`, since 2026-10-05; before that the Finanzamt took them against the debt) and the debt is shared out whole; traders have no income from nowhere.
- **The library's cash pot** (`state["money"]["library"]`, account name `library`): any character borrows coin from it (`town.borrow_coin`, never more than the pot holds) and pays it back into it (`town.repay_coin`, never more than owed). The loan is `library_loan_cp` on the character and grows nightly in `debts.accrue` by `places.LIBRARY_INTEREST_MONTHLY` (6% a month, PLACEHOLDER), weighted by the borrower's moon like the tax debt. Interest is a claim, not coin: it enters the pot only when paid. A borrower's death writes the loan off; the pot keeps their purse.
- **The taps** (admin panel on the town page, actions `issue`, `retire`, `move`): the bank issues new coin into its vault or melts vault coin down, which is the only way the amount of money changes; coin moves between vault, Finanzamt, tills and characters. "Set the purse" and starting gold draw on the vault (`town.fund_purse`) and fail when it is empty.
- **The books** (`westmarch/money.py`): `supply` adds up coin by holder; `record` writes one row per night to the `economy` table (circulating, bank, Finanzamt, all money, town debt, price index) at the end of the nightly update. The price index is today's price over the usual price, averaged over everything on a shop shelf.
- **Luck nudge** (`places.LUCK_NUDGE_CHANCE`, `registry.nudged_luck`): whoever is born with a Luck modifier of +0 has an 80 percent chance that the score was instead drawn from the 3d6 odds above 12 or below 9, each with equal chance, seeded by the name. Applied to all unplayed villagers' rolls and once to every existing character (`registry.apply_luck_rule`, `dcc.rebirth_luck`: augur modifier, Luck left, 1st-level lucky weapon or spell, and augur hit points follow).
- **What a player sees before taking a villager**: five abilities, hit points without the augur's share, gear, coin, debt. Not Luck, not the birth augur.

## 17. Goods as a closed system: materials, making, produce (2026-10-05)

the owner: "nothing appears of vanishes by itself, this goes for goods as well." Equipment "shall be made from either metal, wood, placeholderTechStuff and lastly leather and stuff"; "a blacksmith can only make a 4kg sword made from metal, if he has 4kg of metal in his shop"; tech "can be melted down with a 50% loss each time", metal and leather recycle at 100%; small goods and wood are "produced in small quantities by the villagers".

- **Materials and weights** (`westmarch/materials.py`): four materials with a recycling rate (`MATERIALS`), and a hand-written table of material and kilograms per piece for every rulebook item (`_TABLE`). The rulebook has no weights: every number is a PLACEHOLDER. Things without a line are placed by a word in their name (`_GUESSES`); small goods, services and livestock are made of nothing (`made_of` returns None).
- **Workshop stores**: each shop holds grams per material in `shop["materials"]` (opening amounts in kg under `materials` in `places.PLACES`).
- **Making day** (`town.restock`, on the old delivery rhythm `restock_every`): the keeper makes what is short of the listed quantity, one piece per round, each taking its own weight from the store. When the store is short, spare pieces on the same shelf (second-hand lines first, then what a listed line holds beyond its quantity) are broken up at the material's rate, and only when that yields enough for the piece. Wood is never broken up. The news tells what was made, broken up, and what lacks material.
- **Produce** (`places.PRODUCE`, `daily.produce`, `town.deliver`, `town.deliver_wood`): every night each living, unplayed villager of a fitting occupation brings one piece per good with chance `PRODUCE_CHANCE`, only while a listed shelf is below its quantity. Wood arrives as kg, up to `WOOD_STORE_KG` per workshop that lists wooden things. A villager a player takes over stops producing. No coin moves for produce (open question).
- **No goods from nothing any more**: the five endless lines (candle, chalk, rations, torch, feed) have listed quantities; traders bring only small goods (`brings`, enforced in `daily.arrive`); an unsold purchase no longer leaves a trader's pack, an unscripted trader sells it at a loss after `HOLD_DAYS`.
- **Outside sources**, all the admin's: raw material per place (`town.add_material`, action `material`), the shelf tools (`stock`, `stock_book`). Each is written to `state["goods_log"]`. Still open: a player's "add item" on the sheet creates things freely (it is the only way loot enters today; the owner has loot handling on his list).
- **The books** (`materials.census`): grams per material, raw in workshops and bound in things on shelves, at the library, in trader and character packs. Shown in the admin panel ("Materials in the village"), recorded nightly as JSON in `economy.goods`. Villagers nobody plays hold their rolled things outside this count until taken over.
- **Bug found by the 60-night dry run**: `town.sell` raised "the till cannot pay" after the shelf had already grown, so a refused trader kept the goods and the shelf got a copy. Every refusal now comes before any change; regression test in `test_goods_are_made_from_material_and_nothing_appears`.
- **Page scripts are syntax-checked by the suite** (`test_page_scripts_parse`, needs node): a stray bracket in `dcc_town.js` from the money work took the town page down for about half an hour on 2026-10-05 while the API checks stayed green.

## 24. An economy that moves by itself (2026-10-07 to 10-08)

the owner, after the five weeks of section 23: "too stable, we want trends that follow each other, and natural periodicity", then: trends must be emergent (egg prices follow barley prices because hens need feed and time), not coefficients and not sine waves; swings may be wild as long as they swing back; armor and gear should move +-50 percent inside a week; now and then a good gets stuck at an extreme for weeks, from the actors' own behaviour, and always gets out again; one such event every one to two months.

**What was thrown away.** A first lab version (a per-good trend with persistence and a typed "follow the input good N days later" coefficient) showed lags of 9, 7, 5 and 3 days. Those were the numbers typed in, so the finding was void and the code removed.

**What was built** (`westmarch/economy.py`, switched on by `places.MARKET`, None = the old fixed prices):

- *Scarcity price.* Each night a stocked line moves a fraction of the way to the price its scarcity asks for: shelf against usual stock, to the power 0.9 (staples) or 2.0 (gear), times the whole village's stock against usual to the power 0.5. Range 0,25 to 4 (staples), 0,3 to 3 (gear). The price remembers (speed 0,4 staples, 0,85 gear). Staple waves are scaled to 15 percent, gear waves to 180 percent. A shelf that never runs out has only its wave.
- *Production lags.* A producer decides tonight's amount from the price of the day (price to the power 1,2, between 0,15 and 2,5 times) and the goods arrive `lag` days later: barley 14, hens 21, ale 4, eels 5, hides 7. A recipe is worked only while the product still fetches 85 percent of what its input fetches. What is pending does not count as stock on hand.
- *Fed hens lay.* Eggs: one per fed hen and night (moon-swayed), a dozen at a time; hens that find no barley lay nothing and die as before. A hen keeper keeps six back (`hold`), producers stock up their inputs at any price they can pay (`stock_up`).
- *Households.* Coin pooling in tills (the finding of section 23) now comes back: each night 3 percent of a till's surplus above 30 gp goes to the households (not at all once they hold 800 gp), who eat a diet of staples at the price of the day (fewer when dear, more when they hold more coin) and pay the tills. Coin only moves; the total was one constant number in every run.
- *Wholesale rate.* A shop pays 85 percent of the price for fresh staples (50 percent for everything else). At 50 percent a sack of barley (40 cp) made ale worth 30 cp and the brewer and the eel farmer went broke. The junk shop, which buys without limit, is the producers' emergency outlet; limiting wholesale to listed shelves produced two traps of 95 and 135 days and was reverted.
- *Smith and outfitter lines for wanted raw materials* (scrap, hides) get a usual stock of 10 so the diggers can see when it is full.
- *Traders pay a little tax:* 5 percent of the coin above 5 gp, each night, to the Finanzamt, never more than the debt. All debts were cut 99,9 percent (about 11 gp each) on 2026-10-08; new characters still start at `places.TAX_DEBT`.

**Stuck episodes, what the lab shows** (fresh start, 240-day windows, deterministic; "episode" = a staple at 1,8 times its usual price or more for 14 days or more):

| variant | windows | episodes | lengths (days) | unresolved at the end |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| nothing extra (input starvation of hens, lags, cash squeeze) | 2 | 7 | 16 to 46 | 0 |
| sticky price (a dear staple falls 4 percent a night) | 3 | 15 | 14 to 43 | 0 |
| sticky price + own use | 3 | 16 | 14 to 36 | 0 |
| + traders chasing rising prices | 3 | 19 | 14 to 87 | 3 |
| shipped: sticky price + own use | 3 | 18 | 14 to 35 | 0 |

Own use = a producer who makes a scarce, dear good keeps it back until two days' worth is kept, then releases it all and keeps nothing for ten days. Its first version was a permanent trap (ale dear 217 of 240 days) because the released goods were hoarded again the same night; the rest period is the exit. Momentum buying is left out (an 87-day trap).

**Emergent behaviour, measured on the shipped config:** barley dear for up to 36 days, ale and eggs follow barley's price changes by about 5 to 12 days (correlation 0,1 to 0,2 on changes, 0,5 to 0,7 on levels) while the same-day correlation is about zero; eels run with the Red Lantern and swing 0,6 to 3,6 times; gear moves 2,3 to 3 times (max over min) inside a week, a crowbar 5 times. **Known and open.** Jutta's margin on smoked eel is thin (coal dear, eels cheap). Hides and ale pile up in the shops during gluts. Players' own purchases do not yet move prices (only the shelves do). The live town had piles of staples and broke producers before the switch-on; `economy.fresh_start` settles that once (shelves to usual stock, piles in packs cut to three, a flock of twelve hens for Detlef, 15 gp of working capital per producer moved from the Finanzamt). ## 27. Ageing, families and the matchmaker (2026-10-09)

`westmarch/lives.py`, switched by `places.LIVES` (None = off). Once a month, for everyone nobody plays, the townsfolk included: deaths, weddings, births, coming of age.

- **Death.** Weibull curve (shape 5) around the expected lifespan of the kind: human 60, dwarf 250, halfling 110, elf never. A small accident chance for everyone but elves. What the dead owed goes to the living (`debts.spread`), their coin to the widow or widower, else to the households. Coin is conserved (tested).
- **Romance.** A single adult may find a single adult of the same kind, the other sex, within 15 years (scaled by kind), not parent, child or sibling. They wed at once. Only widows and widowers wed again. Townsfolk and villagers wed each other.
- **Children.** A wed couple may have a child while the mother is in her fertile years (18 to 18 + 0.4 of the kind's lifespan), up to six, scaled by lifespan (an elf has hardly any). Each ability: father's, mother's, their mean or 2d10, one in four each, a point either way with 8 percent. Clamped to 3..18. A child's scores are fixed at birth.
- **Keeping the village near 100.** `steer`: with x = (living / 100 - 1), deaths are multiplied by 1 + x and births by 1 - x. Deaths alone did not hold it: more people means more weddings, so in simulation a village a little above its balance kept growing (to 900) and one a little below kept shrinking (to 30). Deaths are multiplied by 1 + 2x (`steer_deaths`), births by 1 - x. The balance is delicate, and it sits above the target: with `birth` 0.04 a 120-year simulation of the people alone stays at 115 to 135 living. The weibull shape is 7 (deaths bunch late around 60 to 75 for a human), and children die too (`child_death`: about four in ten never see eighteen), which is what lets a couple have three or four children while the village stays small. Elves have a lifespan of 2000 and a thirtieth of a human's birth chance.
- **Coming of age.** At eighteen a 0-level character is rolled with the inherited abilities. The trade is a parent's with 80 percent, else any job on the table, weighted by its roll range. The purse is what the households can spare, so no coin is made.
- **Seats.** Each townsperson holds a seat, a trade others depend on: a shop (the keeper's name) or a trader (the bot's name, in `state["seats"]`). The 19 trades are on the list of jobs a child draws from, with the 80 percent of a parent's trade making the heirs. When a holder dies the eldest adult who has the trade and no seat takes it (and becomes townsfolk, locked). Without one the seat stands empty: the shop is closed (`closed_reason`), the trader idle. A child who comes of age and draws that trade, from the parent's job or at random (a vacant trade counts a hundred slots, `vacant_weight`, not one; at eight, 7 to 10 of the 19 trades stood empty in simulation, at a hundred 1 to 4), takes the seat at once. The herb woman's seat is never vacant: she is an elf. A trader's `who` and `quirk` stay those of the first holder.
- **First month.** Everyone gets a sex (from their given name), a kind (from their trade or the bot's race), an age (uniform from 18 to 80 percent of the lifespan) and a spouse (55 percent), and the married women get up to two children of up to 17. The townsfolk, who have no character, get stand-in scores for their children to inherit.
- **The delver** is a villager (never townsfolk): the best (mean of strength, agility, stamina, luck) of the adults in their prime (18 + 0.45 of the lifespan, at most 120 years' worth). His gold is the old 4 to 6 gp times his score over 10.5 (0.5 to 1.6). A trip kills him with 1 percent at average strength, more for the weak. The next best goes. Garrick Dunmoor remains only as the fallback while ageing is off.
- **Hedwig's hand.** `matches` in her answer: up to three pairs of single adults who could wed. Each month a matched pair weds with 60 percent for up to 24 months. Nobody learns of it. `blocks` is the other hand: up to six pairs of living people (wed or not) whose chance of wedding is multiplied by 0.01 (`block_factor`) while the block stands; it lapses when one dies, and a match on the same pair is dropped. Her report outlines the people (counts, the 19 trades and who holds them or EMPTY, the delver, the last 12 events, her matches); individuals she asks for with `look_up` (person with family, singles by kind, children with their scores and years to eighteen, couples, best for the delver, a trade), at most three questions per round and two rounds, handled by the host script. A child's scores are visible to her and to the admin (register, `inherited`) from birth.
- **Tables.** `people` gains born_day, sex, race, spouse_id, mother_id, father_id, genes, died_day, cause, seat; `life_events` and `lives_monthly` (population, children, couples, births, deaths, weddings, delver) in the campaign database. The register keeps villagers born in play (the file sync no longer deletes them or resets a seated heir).
- **Open.** One seat per trade: further people with the same trade are apprentices (they produce at the people level but run no shop), which waits for the barley fields to change. The economy's household food use does not yet follow headcount.

## 28. The barley fields, the eel ponds and the teaching hand (2026-10-10, night)

`westmarch/fields.py`, switched by `places.FIELDS` (None = barley and eel from `brings` as before). the owner's spec of 10-09 evening; the formula and the eel rule are his to know and nobody else's: they appear in this file and in the code, not in any prompt, report or note.

- **Plots.** 100, one per living person on the first night (the rest to the common pool), fallow. Sown, ripe after 41 nights, then 29 nights' rest. Seed: one sack per 7 plots sown, and it is barley the farmer owns (the owner 10-10): kept back from a harvest, else bought off a shelf with the farmer's own coin (a villager) or taken from the pack (a trader); without it the plot stays fallow. Labour: one person keeps at most 7 plots under barley at once (`work`). Yield 4 sacks × the light factor, a fraction left to chance.
- **The light** (the thing Hedwig is meant to work out): per night ℓ = 0.4 + 0.9·P·(1 − 0.5·D) + 0.3·R with P, D, R the brightness of Pale, Drowned, Red (new 0, crescent 0.25, quarter 0.5, gibbous 0.75, full 1). Factor = mean of ℓ over the 12 nights before the harvest, clamped 0.4..1.6. Scanned over ten years: ℓ averages 0.997, the 12-night mean runs 0.71 to 1.36. Weather does not touch barley any more; only the light does, so the signal is findable. Her report shows each harvest's sacks with the moons' phases at sowing and reaping; the seed note "barley seems to grow better under some skies" is now live in `notebook-seed.txt`.
- **Who tills.** Trader bots with `tills` (Reinhild, Pip): reap into the pack, keep seed back (one sack per 7 plots owned), sow every ready plot the seed allows (naive, all at once), and the old barley `brings` is skipped for them. Villagers whose whole trade is Farmer, Farmhand or Barley farmer till their own plot (the owner 10-10: barley and eel are the only crops; no mushroom farmers, no herders) and deliver the harvest to the shortest shelf for nothing, like PRODUCE. Everyone else's plot lies fallow until a farmer buys it.
- **Land market** (the owner 10-10, replacing my first rule, which priced land off today's barley and locked every farmer out during the shortage). Every owner names their own price, "I'd sell it if you gave me so much right now", crop included (`ask_cp`): a villager asks the usual 16 gp × their own attachment (0.5 to 2, fixed per person) × a need factor (the poor sell cheaper, 0.4 to 1.5) × 3 if farming is their trade, plus 4 sacks at today's barley price when the plot is sown. A farming trader asks what its model set (`ask_field`), else 3 × usual + crop. The common pool asks the usual 16 gp. A buyer pays the ask; the coin goes to the seller's register purse, a trader's purse, or the households. The plain rule buys the cheapest ask when the hands have room, no common plot is free, and the ask is under a year of harvest at today's price. Broke (under 3 gp): sells a fallow plot to the village at 12.80 gp. Model-played farmers see their plots with what is on them and their own prices, the free common plots, and the five cheapest asks.
- **Common land** (the owner 10-10). A farmer whose own plots do not fill their 7 hands sows ready common plots for nothing; the plot is theirs to reap (`worker`), not for sale while worked, and free again after the harvest. A worker who dies leaves the crop to rot.
- **Inheritance.** Each night, a dead owner's plots go to the living spouse, else to the common pool.
- **Ponds.** Jutta has 6 ponds, each giving 2 fresh eels every 20 nights, staggered. Each smoked eel takes one fresh eel besides the coal. **Fat night**: Drowned gibbous or full while neither Pale nor Red is gibbous or full → the catch is ×5. Measured 0.83 such nights a month over ten years; a strict "Drowned full" would give only 0.29, since that moon is full one night in 61. Nothing anywhere says so.
- **Teaching (Hedwig).** `mentor`: up to 4 of {child, trade}; at eighteen the child takes that trade with 85 percent (`mentor_chance`), and if it is the trade of an empty seat, the seat with it. The lesson ends at eighteen; event `taught`. Her report lists `my_mentoring`. Decided by the owner on 10-10 as the answer to the empty seats of the 10-check-in run (Factor, Tinker, Brewer, Blacksmith).
